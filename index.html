How it works

One score. Four responses. No guesswork.

Every change-stream event feeds a damage-weighted score. The score — not a single rule — decides what happens next.

0.35Destructive-command ratedrop, dropDatabase and deleteMany commands per window
0.25Collection churnhow many collections are being hit, and how fast
0.25Data volume at riskvalue-weighted documents — orders count more than inventory
0.15Command rarityrare commands (drop, dropDatabase) score higher than routine writes
0 – 30

Watch

Score is tracked. Nothing is blocked. Normal traffic — bulk inserts, index builds, backups — stays under 30.

≥ 30

Throttle + snapshot

Attacker sessions are throttled (killSessions) and a snapshot is taken on suspicion, so there is something to restore from.

≥ 60

Suspend + quarantine

Writes are suspended and the attacker's role is revoked on the authenticated server — later batches fail with code 13.

≥ 85

Isolate + restore

The database is write-locked (fsyncLock) and the snapshot is restored — staging copy first, rename-swap only on success, verified against recorded counts.

What the lab proved about killing attacks: a drop() is not killable — killOp acknowledged, the drop still completed. killSessions interrupts one operation, not the session. So AurevoDB does not promise to kill a drop. It promises to detect it fast, limit what follows, and restore what was lost. That is the whole design.

Honest limits

What it does not do.

A product page that hides its limits is a brochure. These are measured, and they stay in the report.

  • dropdb prevention is 0% by design. A dropDatabase cannot be killed. The answer is verified fast restore (median 2,669.2 ms) plus least-privilege roles, not prevention.
  • Steady overhead is +242.8% mongod CPU. The ≤+43% target was proven unreachable: priming off still costs +49.1%, and only the 0.02 s priming rate keeps fast-delete alive.
  • Undeclared maintenance looks like an attack. A bulk-delete purge trips the alarm 3/3 times, and an orders drop-and-recreate 3/3 times. Declared maintenance is exempt and scores 0/18 false positives — undeclared work is not.
  • Finding 10 is bounded, not solved. Fast-delete delivery stalls move between seeds (v4: seed 42, v5: seed 777). The stall decomposes into majority-commit visibility and pure change-stream lag inside the server; the 8-minute clustering never reproduced.
  • Damage-prevented % is an upper bound. The emulator is not validated against real ransomware traces, and the lab baselines flatter the system. The report states this in full.
  • Interleaved attacks are the weakest shape at 32.60% median — mixed, pausing attacks are the hardest to contain.
